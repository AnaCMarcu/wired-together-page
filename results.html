RQ1 — Does social plasticity improve cooperative performance?

FindingSocial plasticity is most reliable when coupled into learning: it improves cooperative completion in three of four RL fine-tuning configurations, while at inference time its benefit depends on the backbone.

We compare social plasticity with matched no-graph baselines across three zero-shot VLM backbones (Qwen3.5-2B, Qwen3.5-9B and Gemma-E4B) and four RL fine-tuning configurations (IPPO and MAPPO with Qwen3.5-2B and Gemma-E4B), and on Gemma-E4B against a soft (VillagerAgent-style) and a hard (HMAS-2) central orchestrator.

Social plasticity attains a higher task return than both central orchestrators (548 vs 505 soft and 477 hard) and higher cooperative completion (4.8% vs 4.7% and 3.9%), at the inference compute of the soft orchestrator and a third of the hard orchestrator's, which spends 72.5% of its tokens in orchestrator calls, mostly plan checks and revisions.

RQ2 — How do social interaction regimes shape learned bonds?

FindingBond strength reflects interaction frequency, not necessarily coordination value.

Communication is selected most often and produces the strongest bonds, yet the lowest cooperative completion of any single cue: its bonds are informative about who talks, not who cooperates. Observation and imitation, used far less often, keep cooperative completion close to spatial engagement alone, and no explicit cue improves on it.

Action competitionMore social signals can reduce relational quality through action competition. When several cues compete for a single social-action slot, communication takes 75.7% of social actions in the full comm + obs + imit condition, observation falls to 3.4% and imitation is almost never selected, and the combined-cue conditions reach the lowest cooperative completion (2.4% and 1.5%).

RQ3 — How do memory and bonds carry social experience into a new team?

FindingRetained memory and bonds favour exploitation of the former partner, while bonds alone balance exploitation and exploration.

Pairs trained together in Chambers 1–2 are merged into a six-agent team that restarts at Chamber 3, where each agent's switch opens the door of an agent from a different pair. We vary what the agents keep from their first team; everything keeps updating afterwards. Partner preference is the share of an agent's messages sent to its former partner (chance 0.20); bond-implied is the same share read off the bond table at each episode start.

InterpretationRetaining both episodic memory and bonds favours exploitation of previously successful partners, whereas retaining only bonds balances exploitation and exploration. The bond graph acts as a compact distillation of social experience while remaining plastic enough to rewire toward new collaborators.

Compute and perception

FindingEvery deliberation interval from 2 to 100 steps completes more milestones than no social module (10.3–11.9% against 9.7%), and an interval of 100 (11.0%) still beats the default of 8 (10.3%) at roughly 12× less compute.

Two supporting analyses: how often to run the social module, and how backbone perception relates to zero-shot performance.
